notes_ssa.txt — why qc-- doesn't use SSA internally
Written 2026-08-17, against commit ec9f990.

Question asked: why does qc-- not use SSA to optimize things?

Verified (grepped the tree; no SSA anywhere in the pipeline):
- No module, comment, or .nw chunk implementing SSA construction/destruction
  exists in this fork or (per pad.txt / archi_modules.txt) in upstream.
- The mid-level IR is `front_cfg/` (older Cfg/Dag/Mflow) and `front_zipcfg/`
  (newer zipper-based Zipcfg), both plain CFGs over Rtl, not SSA.
- Optimizations/analyses run as classic iterative dataflow over that CFG via
  a generic `Dataflow` module/functor (forward/backward, may/must lattices;
  see availpass, registerclass, dead-code elim — all integrated per
  [[todo-dir-classification]] in memory).
- Register allocation is Chaitin/Briggs-style graph coloring
  (`regalloc/flowra.ml`, wired in as `Colorgraph.ralloc` at -O3 — see
  [[colorgraph-hang]] memory) plus a separate linear-scan allocator
  (`docs/architecture/index.nw` lists both `ocolorgraph` and `linscan`).
  Neither is SSA-based register allocation (e.g. no SSA-form Briggs/Hack
  style allocator).

Inferred (from project history / the Ramsey-Dias line of later papers, not
re-derived from this repo's code):
- Quick C-- (Ramsey & Lindig) predates SSA's establishment as the default
  mid-level IR in most production/research compilers. Its authors later
  published the "zipper-based CFG" representation (and, in Haskell, Hoopl)
  explicitly as their answer to "how do you get a generic, retargetable
  dataflow-optimization framework without committing to SSA's construction/
  destruction machinery." That design choice predates and likely explains
  qc--'s own architecture.
- qc-- is a retargetable *library/backend*, meant to let arbitrary clients
  (originally Lua scripts; here `fork-tiger`) plug in custom analyses via a
  shared generic dataflow functor operating directly on the CFG — simpler to
  expose across clients than SSA, which needs dominance-frontier/phi-
  placement machinery around every transform and out-of-SSA copy insertion
  before codegen.
- C-- itself is already low-level (explicit registers, calling conventions,
  explicit stack-layout equations resolved early in `Fenv`/`front_target/`).
  SSA's main wins (sparse def-use chains, trivial constant/copy propagation)
  matter most for virtual-temporary-heavy high-level IRs; once you're already
  reasoning about hardware registers and memory aliasing, much of that
  benefit is undercut.
- The project's own stated framing (`docs/architecture/index.nw`) and this
  fork's CLAUDE.md priority ("simplify and get it working end-to-end" over
  "maximally optimizing") both point the same direction: classic CFG +
  dataflow + graph-coloring allocation is simpler to keep working across six
  backends than SSA-based optimization would be, and peak optimization power
  was never the goal.

Net: not an oversight, a coherent (if dated) architectural choice — zipper-
CFG + generic dataflow framework instead of SSA, chosen for retargetability
and client-pluggability, consistent with this fork's own "simplification and
end-to-end correctness over optimization sophistication" priority.
